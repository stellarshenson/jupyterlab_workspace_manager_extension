Workspace manager - design

Design B, refined and aligned with the design system of the jupyterlab-extension skill. DQ-21 and DQ-22 were decided by the Star Colonel on 2026-10-29.

1. What the panel is for

The WORKBENCH sidebar replaces the built-in panel Running Terminals and Kernels. It lists every workspace of the server, and inside each workspace its tabs, terminals and kernels, then every kernel, terminal and language server that runs on the server. It is built for a user who runs several projects at once: each project is one workspace, told apart by a colour strip, and the panel shows what the workspace holds, how old it is and what can be shut down.

2. The panel

The panel is a header band over four section bands. Each band opens and closes its own list.

Panel header

  • The header is a band 24 to 30 px tall on --jp-layout-color2. Its title reads WORKBENCH in --jp-ui-font-size0 at weight 600, uppercase, with 1 px letter spacing, in --jp-ui-font-color1
  • Four 24 px buttons follow, in this order: new workspace (+), filter, show hidden (an eye) and refresh. Each draws a 14 px glyph in --jp-inverse-layout-color3; hover and active paint --jp-layout-color3, and an active glyph takes --jp-brand-color1
  • The + button opens the New workspace dialog: a name, with the keyboard focus, and an optional short description of at most 100 characters, which is the purpose the card shows. Enter creates, Escape closes without a change
  • The filter button shows a field under the header. The field finds anything in the panel: workspace names and purposes, tab paths, terminal process names and kernel types. Hiding the field clears the filter, and a clear button sits inside it
  • The eye includes the hidden workspaces in the list. It shows an eye with a stroke while hidden workspaces are included
  • Refresh reloads workspaces, terminals, sessions and process names; the button is disabled until the requests return. While the panel is visible it reloads by itself every 10 s

Section bands

  • Four bands, in this order: WORKSPACES, KERNELS, TERMINALS and LANGUAGE SERVERS. Each carries its count in brackets after the label: WORKSPACES (6), KERNELS (2), TERMINALS (10), LANGUAGE SERVERS (0)
  • A band is a 24 px surface on --jp-layout-color2; hover --jp-layout-color3. The caret sits on the 4 px inset, the label at 18 px from the edge in --jp-ui-font-size0 at weight 600, uppercase, with 1 px letter spacing, and the count 6 px after the label in --jp-ui-font-color2. No drop shadow
  • Every section starts open. A click on a band opens or closes that section only, and the open state is saved per panel in localStorage, read and written in try/catch
  • Each section's list is its own scroll container (overflow-y: auto; min-height: 0) under a band that sticks at the top (position: sticky; top: 0), with scrollbar-gutter: stable and a height of clamp(5 rows, 33vh, 10 rows). No axis has two scroll containers
  • The end of every band carries a broom button. On KERNELS, TERMINALS and LANGUAGE SERVERS its name is Shut down all; on WORKSPACES its name is Clean up workspaces (DQ-22). The broom is a 22 px square button with a 14 px glyph in --jp-inverse-layout-color3, with no background and no border at rest; disabled it keeps that look at opacity 0.4. It opens a confirmation dialog and stops nothing by itself

Workspace card

  • Each workspace is one card: 2 px corners, a --jp-layout-color1 body, a 1 px --jp-border-color1 border and a header one step up on --jp-layout-color2
  • A 4 px strip is the card's left border, in the workspace's token: rose --jp-error-color1, peach --jp-warn-color1, lemon --jp-warn-color2, mint --jp-success-color1, sky --jp-brand-color1, lavender --jp-info-color1. The strip is the only colour that tells workspaces apart
  • The first header line carries the triangle, the name, the age, the counts and four buttons
  • The name is --jp-ui-font-size1 at weight 400 in --jp-ui-font-color1 (DQ-21). The age reads now, Nm ago, Nh ago, Nd ago, Nmo ago or Ny ago, in --jp-ui-font-color2, and never stands empty
  • The counts are tabs, terminals and kernels, each with its glyph: JupyterLab's tab icon, the extension's terminal outline and JupyterLab's kernel icon. They sit at the right of the second header line
  • The purpose is the short description of the workspace, at most 100 characters, in --jp-ui-font-color2. It shows while the card is open, and a card with no purpose shows no purpose text
  • The four header buttons keep their place on every card: Open workspace, Close all tabs, hide (an eye) and ... (the workspace menu). An action that does not apply is disabled, never removed: Open workspace is disabled on this window's card, and the ... button on a hidden card
  • This window's card carries a 2 px --jp-brand-color2 outline against its edge and no other mark

Rows

  • The open card lists three groups: Tabs, Terminals and Kernels. Each group label is --jp-ui-font-size0 at weight 600, uppercase, with its count after it
  • A KERNELS row in a band carries a status dot with its word (idle, busy or unknown), a name and a time. A row in a card carries a 16 px type icon, a name and a time; a tab row carries the file name alone
  • A row is neutral (DQ-21): the name is --jp-ui-font-size1 at weight 400 in --jp-ui-font-color1; the time, the status word and every detail value are --jp-ui-font-size0 in --jp-ui-font-color2
  • Hover and selection paint --jp-layout-color2. The panel has one selected row
  • Everything else appears on selection: the terminal's process, the kernel type, the document, memory and CPU, a tab's folder, a card's purpose and counts. The detail line shows under the selected row
  • Identifiers - terminal names, kernel ids and paths - are set in --jp-code-font-family
  • Recency is carried by the time label alone: now, Nm ago, Nh ago, Nd ago, Nmo ago, Ny ago (DQ-21)
  • The only brand colour on a row is the focus ring: a 1 px --jp-brand-color1 outline at offset 1 px, and 1 px inside a card, shown for keyboard focus only

4. Dialogs

A dialog has one filled button at most, and Cancel is always a surface button. A destructive action is unfilled, with error-coloured text and border, at the far left (DQ-14).

Delete confirmation

  • Delete workspace sits in the workspace menu, with a bin icon before its label, and is drawn in --jp-error-color1 at rest and while selected (DQ-11)
  • The first click arms it: the item reads Confirm delete and shows a warning icon in place of the bin, in --jp-error-color1. The second click deletes the workspace
  • It never runs on this window's workspace, and the panel refuses it on a workspace another window shows, naming the reason
  • The menu's Shut down terminal and Shut down kernel entries are destructive entries too, drawn in --jp-error-color1

Shut-down confirmation

  • Every broom named Shut down all opens one confirmation before anything stops. The band asks once with the number of items; a row action names the item: "Shut down terminal 7?"
  • A terminal or kernel that another workspace also holds is named with that workspace: "Shut down terminal 7? Workspace auto-6 also holds this terminal."
  • Shut down is the destructive action, unfilled with --jp-error-color1 text and border at the far left; Cancel is the surface button at the right; no button is filled
  • A state line in a dialog has padding 6 px 12 px on --jp-layout-color2 and a 2 px left border in the state colour (DQ-15)
  • Nothing stops until the user confirms

The shut-down confirmation as it renders today: a terminal that another workspace also holds, and the panel's error banner.

Clean-up confirmation

  • The WORKSPACES band carries the same broom as the three bands below it, named Clean up workspaces (DQ-22). It is disabled when there is nothing to clean
  • The broom opens one confirmation. It lists every workspace with its name and what it holds: each tab by path, each terminal and kernel by name
  • Each workspace is marked with its outcome: deleted, emptied for this window's workspace, or kept for a workspace another window shows
  • Clean up deletes every workspace except the one this window shows, closing their tabs. It keeps this window's workspace and empties it, closing its tabs and shutting down its kernels, terminals and language servers. A workspace another window shows is kept and named as kept
  • Clean up is the destructive action, unfilled with --jp-error-color1 text and border at the far left; Cancel is the surface button at the right. Nothing happens without this confirmation

The clean-up confirmation of DQ-22: the count sentence, the list of workspaces with their contents, a destructive Clean up button and Cancel.

How this design reads the decisions

Each point below is a reading this stage made where a decision left a detail open. Each is stated in the criteria named and can be changed there.

  • Selection: a click on a row, or the arrow keys, selects it; the panel has one selected row; it paints --jp-layout-color2 and shows a detail line under its name; the selection survives a reload while its item exists (ACC-LISTS-210-ROW-SELECT). The click still opens the row's item. For a card, selection is being open: the purpose and the counts show while the card is open
  • The name of a kernel row is the file name of the notebook or console it serves, or the kernel id when no session uses it; the kernel type, the document path, memory and CPU are its detail (ACC-KERNEL-48-ROW). The detail line repeats the file name inside the path, which KERN-02 keeps whole
  • The name of a terminal row is terminal <n>; its process is the detail. Terminals have no state, so their rows show no dot. Tab rows show the file name, and the folder is the detail. Rows keep their 16 px type icon, which the decision does not list among the details
  • The tab of a terminal row: a click or Enter on a terminal row, in a card or in TERMINALS, activates the tab of that terminal's running process - the same session name and a session JupyterLab has not disposed - so a tab whose terminal ended never matches, also after a new terminal takes the name; without a match the row opens a tab (ACC-TERM-215-TAB-MATCH)
  • The tab a terminal row keeps: once a row opened or activated a tab, every later click or Enter on that row activates that tab, never a second tab of the terminal; the link ends when the terminal ends or the tab closes, and the row then opens a new tab (ACC-TERM-216-TAB-KEPT)
  • Language server rows keep the replaced panel's label, pylsp (python), and its shutdown button
  • The eye on every card: DQ-12 keeps the eye's place on shown cards too, so on a shown card it hides the workspace, as the brief's "hidden" toggle with an eye icon asks; it is disabled on this window's card; the ... button is disabled on a hidden card (ACC-HIDDEN-213-EYE-TOGGLE)
  • Shut down all sits on the section band, which is --jp-layout-color2, so it is a surface button: --jp-layout-color1 with a 1 px --jp-border-color1 border, as the design system's Buttons section draws a secondary action superseded by DQ-20
  • Section header padding: "the padding of Sections & headers" is 6 px above and below the text, with the caret on the 4 px inset and the label at 18 px, which the same section prescribes
  • Shutdown dialogs have no filled button: Shut down is the destructive action, unfilled and at the far left; Cancel is the surface button
  • The filter still searches process names and kernel types; a row found by its detail shows its name, and selecting it shows the detail
  • The recessed filter field keeps its 4 percent black over --jp-layout-color1, which the design language prescribes for recessed surfaces; it is the one literal colour word left
